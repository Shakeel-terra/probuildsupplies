Why Choose 20mm Porcelain Paving?

Porcelain paving is fired at high temperature until the clay vitrifies — the particles fuse into a dense, glass-hard body with almost no open pore structure. Water absorption sits below 0.5%, which is what makes it frost proof, stain resistant and effectively maintenance free. Natural stone, by contrast, is porous: it drinks in water, holds it through a freeze, and gradually picks up algae, moss and the marks of whatever gets spilled on it.

In practical terms that means a porcelain patio looks much the same in year ten as it did in year one. There is no sealing to do before you lay it and none afterwards. Cleaning is soapy water and a stiff brush. Colour does not fade, because the pattern is fired into the body of the slab rather than printed on the surface.

Every slab we stock is 20mm thick, rated R11 for slip resistance on the matt finish, and CE certified. R11 is the rating you want outdoors in Britain — it holds grip when wet, which matters for a patio that spends half the year damp.

Porcelain Paving vs Indian Sandstone

The most common question we get. Both are good products; they suit different people.

  Porcelain Indian Sandstone
SealingNever neededRecommended, and repeated every few years
Water absorptionUnder 0.5%Considerably higher — it is a natural stone
AppearanceConsistent slab to slabEvery slab different — that is the appeal
UpkeepWash occasionallyWash, treat for algae, re-seal
CuttingNeeds a diamond blade and patienceCuts more easily
LayingFull mortar bed and slurry primer, no exceptionsFull mortar bed, primer advisable

Put simply: choose porcelain if you want a patio that looks the same in ten years with almost no work. Choose Indian sandstone if you want natural variation and the softer, weathered character that only real stone gives. Plenty of customers order samples of both before deciding, which we send free.

How to Lay Porcelain Paving

Porcelain is not laid like concrete slabs, and this is where most DIY patios go wrong. Because the slab is non-porous, mortar cannot soak into it and grip. Laid on five dabs, or straight onto mortar with no primer, the slabs will lift within a season or two.

Sub-base: 100–150mm of compacted MOT Type 1 for a patio, more for a driveway. Falls: around 1:80 away from the house so water runs off. Bedding: a full wet mortar bed, not spot dabs — you want 100% contact under the slab.

The bit people miss: paint a slurry primer onto the back of every slab immediately before setting it. It is the chemical bond between a non-porous slab and the mortar, and skipping it is the single most common cause of failure.

Leave 5mm joints, point with a proper brush-in jointing compound, and cut with a diamond blade — slowly, with water. Porcelain will chip if you rush it.

Frequently Asked Questions
Do you need to seal porcelain paving?
No. Porcelain is vitrified, so its water absorption is under 0.5% and there is nothing for a sealer to soak into. It needs no sealing before laying and none afterwards, which is the main practical difference from natural stone.
Is porcelain paving suitable for driveways?
Yes, at 20mm thick and laid on a properly prepared sub-base with a full mortar bed. A driveway needs a deeper sub-base than a patio — typically 150mm or more of compacted MOT Type 1 — and the slurry primer is essential.
What does R11 anti-slip mean?
R11 is a slip-resistance rating measured on an inclined ramp. It is the grade generally recommended for outdoor use in the UK because it keeps grip when wet. All our matt-finish porcelain paving is rated R11.
How much does porcelain paving cost per m²?
Our range runs from roughly £19 to £25 per square metre with free UK mainland delivery included in the price. Exact prices and pack coverage are shown on each product page.
What size is your porcelain paving?
Most of the range is 600 x 900mm in packs of 40 covering around 21.6m². We also stock 600 x 600mm in packs of 60, mix patio packs of 72 pieces, and a 16mm option in 600 x 900mm.
Can you cut porcelain paving with an angle grinder?
Yes, with a diamond blade rated for porcelain, cutting slowly and preferably wet. Porcelain is far harder than concrete or sandstone and will chip if you force it.
Do I need a slurry primer for porcelain paving?
Yes, and it is the step most often skipped. Because porcelain is non-porous, mortar cannot key into it. A slurry primer painted onto the back of each slab immediately before laying creates the bond. Without it, slabs work loose within a season or two.
Porcelain or Indian sandstone — which is better?
Neither is better; they suit different priorities. Porcelain needs no sealing, does not stain, and looks the same in ten years. Indian sandstone has natural variation and a softer character, but needs sealing and periodic cleaning.
How much porcelain paving do I need?
Measure the area in square metres and add about 10% for cuts and breakages. Each product page shows the coverage per pack, so you can work out how many packs that comes to.
Does porcelain paving fade in sunlight?
No. The colour and pattern are fired into the body of the slab rather than printed onto the surface, so it does not fade, and porcelain's low porosity means it resists staining and algae far better than natural stone.
